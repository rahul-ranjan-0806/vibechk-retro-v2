The recommendation

Kill the global Candidates tab. Four of five advisors converged independently, and all five peer reviewers judged the First Principles reframe (B) as the strongest analysis: the duplication is a data-model problem leaking into nav, and Home is already declared the cross-role triage surface in CLAUDE.md. A second cross-role surface is a vote of no-confidence in Home.

The Expansionist's argument (talent pooling, Alt portfolio, hiring intelligence) is real but misdirected — those features have different shapes (a Talent Pool tab, an Alt Activity surface) and shouldn't squat the slot of a feature that doesn't exist yet.

The one thing to do first

Before deleting code, add one paragraph to CLAUDE.md declaring "the unit of work is the dilemma; Home owns cross-role surfaces; per-role pages own per-role work." That single decision resolves the model confusion that produced the duplicate tab — and it locks in the IA before the next nav debate recreates the same redundancy elsewhere.

Where the council agrees

  • The global tab is read-only — every click ends with the user leaving for the role page.
  • Home already owns cross-role triage by spec; a second one undermines it.
  • Two surfaces named "Candidates" is a naming collision an outsider can't resolve without trial-and-error.
  • Maintaining duplicate filter UIs and row components is real ongoing tax (every change is 2×).

Where the council clashes

Expansionist vs everyone else on whether the slot is worth holding for future features. The Expansionist sees strategic moat; the other four see a placeholder for things that don't exist yet and will likely take a different shape.

First Principles vs the rest on which surface is actually redundant. If Home owns triage and Alt owns portfolio, the per-role Candidates sub-tab might be as suspect as the global one.

Blind spots the peer review caught

  • Candidate identity is undefined. If one person applies to two roles, are they one record or two? Determines whether "global candidates" is even coherent.
  • Talent pool is a different object. The strongest pro-keep argument (rejected-for-A, perfect-for-B) belongs in a future Talent Pool surface — not a read-only flat all-candidates table.
  • Migration cost was unaddressed. No one asked who currently uses /candidates, whether the team's go-live checklist references it, or if it was a deliberate vs leftover decision.

Full advisor responses

The Contrarian Kill

Kill it. And while you're at it, interrogate why you built it in the first place — that's the real bug.

The global Candidates tab is a prop. It looks powerful in a demo ("see every candidate in one view!") but it does nothing the product actually needs. Three problems you're glossing over:

  1. It's a read-only dead-end. Every click on this tab ends with the user leaving it for the role page where the actual work happens.
  2. Home already does the only job that matters. "Needs your call" surfaces cross-role dilemmas. The global Candidates tab competes with Home and loses.
  3. It contradicts the product thesis. A global flat list is the pre-Alt mental model — the spreadsheet view ATS users are escaping. It tells users "you should still be reviewing everyone."

Cut it. If cross-role triage matters later, it belongs as a Home widget, not a nav slot.

The First Principles Thinker Reframe → Kill

You're asking the wrong question entirely. The real question isn't "global tab or no global tab." It's: what is the primary unit of work in this product?

If the unit is the role → global tab is a category error, kill it.
If the unit is the candidate → kill the per-role sub-tab and make role pages link to a filtered global view.
You're trying to have it both ways and that's why you've built the same table twice.

Given everything else in your spec, the unit of work you've actually committed to is the dilemma. Home is already the cross-cutting triage surface (CLAUDE.md says so). The global Candidates tab isn't a second triage surface — it's a confession you don't trust Home to do its job. Fix Home or kill the tab. Don't ship both.

The Expansionist Keep

Keep the global Candidates tab. You're undervaluing what it becomes. Three things get unlocked:

Talent pooling. Candidates rejected for Role A are often perfect for Role B. An Alt that surfaces this is a wedge into recruiting CRM territory.

The Alt's portfolio view. "Your Alt screened 47 people this week, flagged 6, you decided on 4." That's the dopamine hit that makes founders trust the Alt with more roles.

Hiring intelligence. Pattern recognition only works across roles: "candidates from these schools convert 3x," "your Alt's leniency drifts on senior roles." That's the founder-taste-amplifier vision.

Linear keeps Inbox and Issues as separate top-level nav even though Inbox is "just" a filtered view — because the cognitive mode is different. Don't kill the slot. Make it carry weight.

The Outsider Kill

Walking in cold: I see "Candidates" in the top nav and "Roles" right next to it. I click Candidates first because that's the noun I care about. Land on a flat table, click a row, get a thin slide-over, feel underwhelmed. Then I find Roles → role → Candidates and get the actual rich panel.

Now I'm confused. Why are there two Candidates pages? Which is the "real" one? That's the killer — naming collision in top nav is the kind of thing users never quite forgive. Cut it. If someone wants to scan everyone, that's a filter view inside Roles or a saved view on Home, not a nav slot competing with the richer page that shares its name.

The Executor Kill

Kill it. Monday morning:

  1. Delete the Candidates route from the nav.
  2. On Home, add a "View all candidates needing review" link on the "Needs your call" section.
  3. Add a Cmd+P shortcut for global candidate search/jump — typeahead by name, jumps into the role's detail panel.
  4. Grep for /candidates references and CandidatesPage imports; redirect them all.

2–3 hours of work. The real cost of keeping both isn't the nav slot — it's divergence drift over the next 6 months as you maintain two filter UIs and two row components. If cross-role triage turns out to be a real need, you'll know within two weeks of real users; add it back then with evidence.

Peer review highlights

Strongest response (5/5): First Principles. It refuses the framing and forces the upstream question, then uses CLAUDE.md against itself to land the killer point.

Biggest blind spot (5/5): Expansionist. Conflates "we might want X someday" with "keep this specific tab now" — defending a slot, not a feature.

What all five missed:

  • Candidate identity model — if one person applies to two roles, are they one record or two?
  • The role-scoped sub-tab might be the redundant one, not the global one (council assumed role-scoped is sacred).
  • Migration cost — bookmarks, Slack-shared URLs, the team's go-live checklist.
  • Was the global tab a deliberate Abhishek/Kinnari decision or an IA leftover from before Home existed?
  • Talent Pool is a different object (rejected-but-revisitable) — not a nav redesign.
